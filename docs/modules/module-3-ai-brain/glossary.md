# Glossary: Isaac/NVIDIA Robotics Terms

This glossary provides definitions for key NVIDIA Isaac and robotics terms used throughout the AI-Robot Brain module.

## A

**AI Inference**: The process of using a trained neural network to make predictions on new data, often accelerated using NVIDIA GPUs.

**Accelerated Computing**: Using specialized hardware (like GPUs) to accelerate compute-intensive tasks such as deep learning inference.

**Action**: A ROS 2 construct for long-running tasks with feedback, similar to services but with streaming updates.

## D

**Deep Learning**: A subset of machine learning using neural networks with multiple layers to extract features and make predictions.

**Domain Randomization**: A technique in synthetic data generation where environment parameters are randomized to improve model generalization.

## E

**Edge AI**: Artificial intelligence algorithms processed locally on hardware rather than in the cloud, often used in robotics for real-time inference.

## G

**GPU (Graphics Processing Unit)**: Specialized hardware for parallel processing, commonly used for accelerating AI computations.

**Ground Truth**: Accurate reference data used for training and evaluating AI models, often generated synthetically in Isaac Sim.

## I

**Isaac AI**: NVIDIA's platform for developing AI applications for robotics, including Isaac Sim, Isaac ROS, and Isaac Apps.

**Isaac ROS**: A collection of accelerated perception and navigation packages that bridge Isaac Sim to real-world ROS 2 applications.

**Isaac Sim**: NVIDIA's robotics simulation application based on Omniverse, providing photorealistic simulation and synthetic data generation.

**Isaac ROS Bridge**: Components that translate between Isaac Sim's representation and ROS 2 message formats.

## L

**LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging)**: A remote sensing method using light in the form of a pulsed laser to measure distances.

**Localization**: The process of determining a robot's position and orientation within a known map.

## M

**Machine Learning**: A type of AI that learns patterns from data without explicit programming for each task.

**Mapping**: Creating a representation of the environment, often used for navigation and localization.

## N

**Nav2 (Navigation 2)**: ROS 2's navigation stack providing path planning and execution capabilities.

**NVIDIA Jetson**: A family of AI computing devices designed for robotics and edge AI applications.

**NVIDIA RTX**: Graphics cards with ray tracing and AI acceleration capabilities.

## O

**Omniverse**: NVIDIA's platform for 3D design collaboration and simulation, underlying Isaac Sim.

## P

**Perception Pipeline**: A series of processing steps to extract meaningful information from sensor data.

**Photorealistic Rendering**: Rendering techniques that produce images indistinguishable from real photographs.

## S

**SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping)**: The computational problem of constructing or updating a map of an unknown environment while simultaneously keeping track of an agent's location.

**Synthetic Data**: Artificially generated data that mimics real-world data, often used for training AI models.

**Sensor Fusion**: The process of combining data from multiple sensors to obtain more accurate and reliable information.

## V

**VSLAM (Visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping)**: SLAM using visual sensors (cameras) as the primary input.

**Virtual Sensor**: A simulated sensor in Isaac Sim that generates synthetic sensor data.

## X

**XR (Extended Reality)**: An umbrella term encompassing virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and mixed reality (MR).
